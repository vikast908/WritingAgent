## When to apply
When a protagonist with a defined trait (e.g., 'by-the-book,' 'passive,' 'cautious') makes a sudden decision (e.g., joining a dangerous program, sabotaging equipment).

## Technique
- Before the decision, insert a beat showing the trait straining against the new circumstance (e.g., Lena's fingers trembling as she mentally recites carrier regulations).
- Anchor the emotional reaction to the blueprint's specified tone (e.g., 'terrified, not philosophical') by using short, visceral internal monologue (e.g., 'This is wrong. I can't breathe.')
- If the decision is to cooperate, frame it as capitulation, not agreement; if it's to rebel, show a snap judgment born of panic, not a sudden shift in values.

## Anti-pattern it replaces
Letting a by-the-book character become reflective or sentimental under high stakes without first showing the fear and internal conflict that undermines the noir dread.
