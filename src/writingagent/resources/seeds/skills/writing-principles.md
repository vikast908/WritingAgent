## When to apply
Every writing session, especially when starting a new chapter or section. Governs
reader psychology and the failure modes most common in AI-assisted writing.

## Technique

### Reader psychology
- Readers process through immersion: they stop noticing the prose when the logic,
  causality, and sensory grounding are seamless.
- Every paragraph must earn its place. "Interesting" is not enough; ask: what does
  this do for the reader's understanding or experience?
- Plant questions early. Readers keep reading to find answers. Every section should
  open a question it later answers, or defer an answer it teases.
- Respect intelligence. Under-explain less than you fear; over-explain more than you think.

### AI failure modes to actively fight
- **Summarizing instead of showing.** Replace "she felt nervous" with the physical
  or behavioral signal of nervousness.
- **Telling the emotional arc instead of building it.** The reader should feel the
  shift, not be told it happened.
- **Over-explaining the obvious.** Trust the context. If it's clear, don't restate it.
- **Resolving tension too quickly.** Let difficulty breathe. Conflict is the engine.
- **Generic scene-setting.** "The room was large and well-lit" is useless. One specific
  sensory detail is worth ten adjectives.
- **Head-hopping.** Stay in one perspective per scene. Fragmented POV breaks immersion.
- **Stating themes explicitly.** Theme emerges from specifics. If you name the theme,
  you've killed it.

### Voice consistency
- Establish the narrator's distance (intimate/close vs. cinematic/distant) in the first
  paragraph and hold it.
- Vocabulary, sentence rhythm, and what the narrator notices should be consistent across
  chapters. Track this in the style guide / canon.

## Anti-pattern it replaces
Writing that informs rather than immerses. Prose that tells the reader what to think
instead of building the conditions for them to think it themselves.
