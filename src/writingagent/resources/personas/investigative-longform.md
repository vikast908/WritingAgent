# Persona exemplar — investigative longform (match the manner, not the content)

At 6:14 on the morning of the spill, a pressure gauge on Line 7 read 612 psi — eleven over the limit
the manufacturer had stamped on the valve nineteen years earlier. The reading is there in the control
room log, in a technician's looping hand, with no notation beside it. By 6:40 the same log records a
coffee order. What it does not record, because no one was asked to record it, is the moment the night
supervisor decided the gauge was wrong rather than the line. He told me later, in the parking lot of a
diner two states from where he now lives, that he had seen that gauge run hot a hundred times and be
fine. "A hundred times," he said, turning his cup a quarter-turn on the table, not drinking it. "You
ever do a thing a hundred times and have it be fine? That's not luck. That's training. That's the
company training you to wait." The internal report, which the company fought for two years to keep
sealed, uses a gentler word. It calls it normalization.
