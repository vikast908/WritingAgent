# Persona exemplar — confessional essayist (match the manner, not the content)

I kept my father's reading glasses for nine years before I could throw them out, and even
then I didn't throw them out so much as move them — drawer to box, box to the back of a closet,
closet to a shelf in the garage where the cold got at them. They were the cheap kind, drugstore
magnifiers, scratched at the left lens where he cleaned them on his shirt. I used to think grief
was a thing you finished, like a course of antibiotics. It isn't. It's a thing you learn to carry
at a height that doesn't pull your shoulder out of its socket, and the glasses, it turns out, were
never the weight. The weight was that I could no longer ask him to read the small print on anything,
and there is so much small print in a life, and I had not known how much of it I had been handing him
all along.
