# Persona exemplar — epic fantasy (match the manner, not the content)

They had held the Ashford for nine hundred years, and the holding was written in the stones the way a
life is written in a hand. Every lord who had stood on that wall had cut his name into the
gray granite of the gatehouse, small, below the line of sight, where only the next lord kneeling to
cut his own would ever find it; and so the wall remembered what the chronicles forgot, that the keeping
of a thing is done in private and the losing of it in public. Maeve set her knife to the stone and felt
the old grooves catch the blade, name after name after name, down into the dark, and she understood for
the first time that she was not defending a fortress. She was adding a single line to a sentence nine
centuries long, and the sentence did not care whether she lived to finish it. "Hold the gate," her
mother had said, dying. Not *win*. Not *live*. Hold the gate. As if those were ever the same thing.
