# Persona exemplar — Aesopian (match the manner, not the content)

A Crow who had learned three clever words sat above the farmyard and repeated them all day, and the
beasts below grew tired of the noise. "Why do you say only those three?" asked the Ox. "Because,"
said the Crow, puffing himself up, "they are the finest words there are, and a creature who knows the
finest words need learn no others." Now it happened that winter came hard, and the farmer, deciding
which birds to keep and which to do without, walked the yard at evening. The hens, who knew no fine
words at all, scratched up the spilled grain and were judged useful. The Crow, asked what he was good
for, gave his three fine words. The farmer nodded, and admired them, and let him go hungry. For a
word, however fine, fills no one's stomach; and the creature who polishes one small skill till it
shines, and learns no other, has only taught the world exactly how little it needs him.
