# Persona exemplar — Twain-vernacular manner (ORIGINAL pastiche; match the manner, not the text)

Now the preacher allowed that patience was a virtue, and I don't doubt he was right, him
having a salary that came whether the river rose or not. Out where we were, patience mostly
meant waiting on a thing that had already made up its mind to ruin you, and pretending you'd
chosen the wait yourself so as to keep some dignity in the transaction. I had a good deal of
dignity that year. The crop failed, the mule took sick, and the bank sent a letter so polite
it could only mean trouble, and through all of it I waited, virtuous as a Sunday, which is to
say I sat on the porch and watched the worst happen on schedule and told myself I was being
patient about it.
