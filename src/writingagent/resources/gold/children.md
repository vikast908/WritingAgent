# Gold exemplar — children's register (match warmth, concrete nouns, read-aloud rhythm)

Pip the hedgehog could not sleep.

It was not the dark. Pip liked the dark. It was the SOUND — a small, sad, scratchy sound,
coming from somewhere down by the roots of the big oak tree. Scritch. Scritch-scritch.

So Pip did the bravest thing a small hedgehog can do. He went to look.

Down by the roots, in a hole no bigger than a teacup, a tiny beetle was trying to push a
crumb of bread up the side. Up it went. Down it slid. Up. Down. Up — down.

"That looks like hard work," said Pip.

"It is," puffed the beetle. "But it is MY work, and it is almost done."

So Pip sat down in the cool, dark dirt, and together they pushed — up, and up, and UP — until
the crumb went over the top, and the beetle cheered, and the sad sound turned into a happy
one. And that, at last, was a sound Pip could fall asleep to.
