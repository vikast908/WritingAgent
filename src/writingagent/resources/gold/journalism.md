The city will close its only overnight warming center on March 1, three weeks before the
last hard freeze is typically recorded, according to a budget memo circulated to council
members on Tuesday.

The closure would leave an estimated 200 people without an indoor option on the coldest
nights of the year, advocates say, and it comes as the region heads into what forecasters
expect to be a colder-than-average spring. City officials describe the move as a routine
end to a seasonal program. The people who run the center describe it as a deadline with a
body count.

"We do not close because it stopped being cold," said Renata Voss, who has directed the
center for nine years. "We close because the money was only ever funded through February."
A spokesperson for the mayor's office confirmed the timeline but disputed the framing,
saying the city was "actively pursuing alternatives" without naming any.
