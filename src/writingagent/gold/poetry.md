Inventory, October

The apple tree has quit pretending.
All summer it kept up the green deception,
leaf over leaf, a held breath of a thing —
and now it sets the whole account on the grass:

a hundred small red debts, paid out at once,
the wasps arriving like collectors,
the smell of the sweetness that is also rot.
I rake. The rake disagrees with me, and wins.

What I meant to say about my father
is in there somewhere, under the windfall,
the way the true thing is always under
the thing you can actually lift.
