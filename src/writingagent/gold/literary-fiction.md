# Gold exemplar — literary fiction register (match scene, sense, and restraint)

The kettle ticked as it cooled. Mara stood at the window with both hands around a mug she
had not filled, watching the neighbor's dog work the length of its chain and give up at the
same patch of dead grass it gave up on every morning. Behind her the floorboard spoke —
the third one from the door, the one her father had meant for years to fix and never did,
and now never would.

She did not turn around. If she turned around he would be in the doorway in his coat, and
then it would be a morning with a leaving in it, and she wanted one more minute of the
ordinary kind. The dog circled. The radiator knocked twice and held its breath. Outside, a
car she didn't recognize slowed at the end of the street, idled, and moved on, and only
when its sound was gone did she notice she had been counting the seconds, the way you count
thunder, waiting to learn how far away the thing was that you already knew was coming.
