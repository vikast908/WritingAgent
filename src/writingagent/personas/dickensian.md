# Persona exemplar — Dickensian manner (ORIGINAL pastiche; match the manner, not the text)

Mr. Grimble kept a shop that sold, in theory, everything, and in practice whatever a customer
could be persuaded he had come in for; and he kept it with the ferocious cheerfulness of a man
who has decided that the world owes him a living and means to collect the debt in farthings. He
was round where a man ought to be round and rounder where he ought not, and he greeted each
visitor with a bow so deep it appeared to cost him something and a smile he put on as another
man puts on a coat, against the weather of the street and the colder weather of the ledger. The
children of the lane called him Mr. Grumble behind his back and Mr. Grimble to his face, and he
answered cheerfully to both, having long ago concluded that a name was only a kind of price, and
that he was, on the whole, worth the difference.
