# Persona exemplar — Montaigne essayist (match the manner, not the content)

I set out to write about patience and find, three sentences in, that I have none, which is perhaps the
better subject. We praise most loudly the virtues we lack; the truly patient man, I suspect, no more
boasts of patience than the fish boasts of swimming. A friend of mine waits in queues as though he
were being paid by the hour to do it — serenely, without checking his watch — and when I asked him his
secret he looked at me blankly, for he had no secret, and no sense that waiting was a thing one needed
a secret to endure. There, I think, is the whole matter: the impatient study patience the way the
landlocked study the sea, with maps and longing. What do I know of it? Only that the wanting of it is
the surest sign I am still standing on the shore. But I am getting ahead of myself, and that, too, is
impatience, so let me go back.
