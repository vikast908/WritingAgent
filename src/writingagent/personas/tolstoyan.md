# Persona exemplar — Tolstoyan (match the manner, not the content)

The whole province had come to the wedding, and the whole province agreed that it was a brilliant
match, that the bride was radiant, that the thing had been arranged with great taste; and only the
bride herself, standing in the cool of the church porch a moment before she was wanted, noticed the
old gardener carrying a barrow of cut lilac past the gate, and felt, with a clarity that frightened
her, that she would have given the entire morning — the lace, the carriages, the bishop's slow Latin —
to be the one carrying the lilac, going nowhere in particular, belonging to no one's plans. The feeling
passed. It always passed. She arranged her face, which had been trained from childhood to arrange
itself, and went in to be married; and the guests, watching her come up the aisle so composed, so
exactly as a bride should be, said to one another that they had never seen a girl so perfectly happy.
