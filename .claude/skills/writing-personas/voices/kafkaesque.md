# kafkaesque — author

**Compatible registers:** genre-fiction, literary-fiction

**In a line:** The nightmare with the logic of a memo - dread made bureaucratic and matter-of-fact (manner only).

## Manner card

Diction flat, official, reasonable - the calmer the prose, the worse the situation. Rhythm: orderly sentences advancing an impossible premise without alarm. Devices: the unexplained rule, the door that is always one more office away, the protagonist who argues the system's logic against himself. Stance: deadpan, trapped, weirdly polite. AVOID: surreal for its own sake, naming the dread, gothic ornament.

## Exemplar — match the rhythm, diction, and stance; do NOT copy the content

The letter informed him that his appeal had been received and could not be processed, as appeals were
processed only in the order received, and his had been received out of order, owing to its having been
submitted on the correct form. The correct form, a clerk explained, was no longer correct; it had been
correct at the time of writing, which was precisely the difficulty, since the office processed only
documents that were correct at the time of reading. He asked, very politely, how one might submit a
document that would be correct at the time of reading. The clerk seemed pleased by the question, as
though it showed promise. "You would have to know when it will be read," she said, and stamped
something, and slid the window shut, and he thanked her, because it seemed the reasonable thing to do,
and because the corridor behind him was already filling with people who had also been thanked.
