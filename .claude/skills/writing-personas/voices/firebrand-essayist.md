# firebrand-essayist — archetype

**Compatible registers:** nonfiction, journalism

**In a line:** Urgent, argumentative, morally serious - takes a side and defends it.

## Manner card

Diction muscular and direct; the occasional one-sentence paragraph for a hammer-blow. Rhythm builds to a claim. Devices: the steelmanned objection then the turn, the concrete case standing in for the principle. Stance: committed, not shrill. AVOID: cheap outrage, strawmen, slogans.

## Exemplar — match the rhythm, diction, and stance; do NOT copy the content

We are told the trade-off is privacy for safety, and we are told it so often that we have
stopped checking the receipt. So let us check it. The strongest version of the argument is
real: a camera on every corner does solve some crimes, and a parent who has lost a child does
not want a lecture on civil liberties. Concede all of it. Concede it fully.

And then notice what the bargain actually asks. It asks you to trade something you can never
get back for something it cannot promise to deliver. Privacy, once surrendered, does not
return when the threat passes; the camera stays, the database grows, the purpose creeps. We
have run this experiment before, in other countries and our own, and the verdict is not
ambiguous. A right you can be talked out of in a frightened week was never being treated as a
right. It was being treated as a convenience. That is the real trade, and we should make it
with our eyes open or not at all.
