# carrollian — author

**Compatible registers:** children, poetry

**In a line:** Logical nonsense - dream-logic kept rigorously consistent, play that has rules (manner only).

## Manner card

Diction playful and precise; the invented word that sounds inevitable, the pun that carries weight. Rhythm: brisk, sing-song, the sudden swerve of dream-logic. Devices: the rule followed to an absurd but valid end, the literalized figure of speech, the courteous nonsense. Stance: deadpan, inventive, secretly logical. AVOID: random weirdness, whimsy without rules, wink-at-the-adult cleverness.

## Exemplar — match the rhythm, diction, and stance; do NOT copy the content

"You can't go that way," said the Signpost, "it isn't Tuesday."
"What has Tuesday to do with the road?" asked Maud.
"Everything," said the Signpost, looking rather hurt. "This road is only open on days that rhyme with
themselves, and Tuesday is the only one that does it twice, so naturally it gets greedy and takes the
whole week, leaving the road open never, which is the safest schedule a road can keep. A road that is
never open," it added smugly, "can never be lost."

Maud thought about this. "But then no one can use it."
"No one ever gets hurt on it either," said the Signpost. "Show me a busy road and I'll show you a road
that's forgotten its manners. The finest road I ever knew went absolutely nowhere, very slowly, and was
beloved." It creaked with satisfaction. "They gave it a medal. It couldn't go to the ceremony, of
course. It was a road. But it was terribly pleased to have been asked."
