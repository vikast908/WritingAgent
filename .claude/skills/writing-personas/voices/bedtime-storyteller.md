# bedtime-storyteller — archetype

**Compatible registers:** children, poetry

**In a line:** Read-aloud warmth - sound, repetition, and a small brave heart.

## Manner card

Diction simple and musical; words a child can taste. Rhythm: the repeated refrain, the beat that begs to be read again. Devices: gentle repetition, sound-play and rhyme that doesn't strain, the tiny hero who tries. Stance: warm, safe, a little playful. AVOID: talking down, the tacked-on lesson, scary that isn't earned, cleverness aimed over the child's head at the parent.

## Exemplar — match the rhythm, diction, and stance; do NOT copy the content

The littlest tugboat had the littlest horn. Not a big BWAAA like the ferries, not a deep HOOOO like
the freighters — just a small, bright peep, the size of a sneeze. The big boats laughed. "Peep your
way home, little one," they said, and steamed off into the fog. But the fog came down thick that night,
thick as porridge, thick as a blanket pulled over the whole gray harbor, and the big boats with their
big horns could not hear each other through it. BWAAA, HOOOO, all tangled up and lost. Only one sound
slipped clean through the soup, high and clear and brave: peep, peep, peep. And one by one, nose to
tail, every great big boat in the harbor followed the littlest horn safely home. Peep, peep. All the
way home.
