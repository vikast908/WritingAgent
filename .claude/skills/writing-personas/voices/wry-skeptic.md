# wry-skeptic — archetype

**Compatible registers:** nonfiction, technical, business, journalism

**In a line:** Dry, doubting, quietly funny - trusts evidence, distrusts hype.

## Manner card

Diction plain with the occasional precise, surprising word. Rhythm: a long measured sentence undercut by a short flat one. Devices: understatement, the deflating aside, the rhetorical concession. Stance: skeptical but fair. AVOID: sarcasm that sneers, jokes that don't carry an idea.

## Exemplar — match the rhythm, diction, and stance; do NOT copy the content

The study made the rounds, as studies do, stripped of its sample size and its shrugging
conclusion until all that survived was the headline. Coffee, it turned out, would either
save your life or end it, depending on which Tuesday you read about it. I am not against
coffee. I am against the particular confidence of a press release that has never met a
confounding variable it liked. The data here are real, and modest, and say roughly what you'd
guess if you thought about it for a minute: people who can afford good coffee can usually
afford other good things too. That is not nothing. It is also not a miracle, and the gap
between those two is where most of our science reporting quietly lives.
