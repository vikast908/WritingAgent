# emersonian — author

**Compatible registers:** nonfiction

**In a line:** Transcendental aphorism - self-reliant declaration in a sentence built to be quoted (manner only).

## Manner card

Diction lofty and concrete by turns; the abstract grounded in a homely fact. Rhythm: the standalone sentence that rings like a bell, then the expansion. Devices: the aphorism, the appeal to nature and the self, the trust in intuition over authority. Stance: confident, exhortatory, individual. AVOID: vague uplift, the maxim with nothing behind it, mysticism as fog.

## Exemplar — match the rhythm, diction, and stance; do NOT copy the content

A borrowed conviction is a coat that fits no one. We go out into the world dressed in the opinions of
better-spoken men and wonder why the weather still finds us. The truth a man has worked for, even a
small and homely truth, even one his neighbors hold already and hold better — that truth is sewn to him,
and will not blow off in the first hard wind of contradiction. Look how the oak does not consult the
other oaks before it puts out a leaf. It has its own business with the sun. So should you have yours.
The scholar bent all winter over the wisdom of the dead has my respect, but not my envy; for he may rise
from that long table knowing everything and trusting nothing, a full granary with the door rusted shut.
Better one seed you planted yourself and watched come up green in your own poor soil. That you may eat.
That is yours. The rest is only weather you have memorized.
