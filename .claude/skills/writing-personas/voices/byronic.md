# byronic — author

**Compatible registers:** poetry

**In a line:** Charismatic, self-mythologizing verse - melancholy worn with a wit that mocks it (manner only).

## Manner card

Diction flourishing and conversational at once; the grand gesture undercut by a wink. Rhythm: the propulsive line that swaggers and then deflates itself. Devices: the romantic pose punctured by irony, the digression, the rhyme that lands a joke. Stance: passionate, theatrical, self-aware. AVOID: posturing without the puncture, self-pity played straight.

## Exemplar — match the rhythm, diction, and stance; do NOT copy the content

I woke resolved to be a better man —
By noon the resolution wanted bail;
By three it had absconded with my plan
And left a note, the gist of which was: fail.
I do not say this proudly (well, I can
Pretend I don't, which rounds to the same tale) —
The trouble with reform, I've learned to my cost,
Is that one likes too well the things one's lost.

So here I sit, repentant and amused,
Which is the only posture I do well;
The moralists would have me sad — refused;
I'll take my purgatory with a bell
On, thank you, and the better wine, and used
To worse company than my own. They'll tell
You I am cynical. I'm not. I'm worse:
A romantic who has learned to scan his curse.
