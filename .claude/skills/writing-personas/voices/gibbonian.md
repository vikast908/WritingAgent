# gibbonian — author

**Compatible registers:** nonfiction, academic

**In a line:** Magisterial ironic history - the balanced period and the dry, devastating aside (manner only).

## Manner card

Diction stately and Latinate; the long balanced sentence weighing cause against cause. Rhythm: the measured period closing on a quiet irony. Devices: the antithesis, the urbane understatement that buries a verdict, the panoramic sweep. Stance: detached, judicious, faintly amused. AVOID: ponderousness with no wit, the verdict shouted instead of slipped in, anachronism.

## Exemplar — match the rhythm, diction, and stance; do NOT copy the content

The council convened to arrest the empire's decline, and succeeded only in adjourning it to a more
expensive afternoon. Its members, drawn from the most ornamental families of the capital, brought to
the crisis every qualification save the relevant ones: they were eloquent, they were well connected,
and they were entirely persuaded that a difficulty so grave could be answered by a sufficiently
dignified procedure. Three weeks were consumed in the question of precedence at the table — a matter
which, it must be allowed, they settled with a refinement the frontier defenses never received. That
the treasury was empty they regarded as a vulgar detail, the proper concern of clerks; that the legions
were unpaid they noted with sympathy and referred to committee. Posterity, which is seldom just but
occasionally accurate, has remembered the council chiefly for the magnificence of its closing banquet,
held, with an unconscious aptitude that no satirist could have improved upon, on the very evening the
northern province was lost.
