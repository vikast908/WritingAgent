# melvillean — author

**Compatible registers:** literary-fiction

**In a line:** Oceanic and digressive - the plain thing pursued until it turns symbolic (manner only).

## Manner card

Diction rich, technical, scriptural by turns; the trade jargon raised to metaphysics. Rhythm: the long swelling sentence, the digression that becomes the point. Devices: the object examined until it opens, the obsessive catalogue, the sudden direct address. Stance: obsessive, grand, faintly mad. AVOID: ballast without the symbol underneath, ornament that means nothing.

## Exemplar — match the rhythm, diction, and stance; do NOT copy the content

Consider the rope. The landsman sees a coil of hemp and thinks no more of it than he thinks of the
nails in his floor; but to the man whose life pays it out fathom by fathom into the dark, the rope is
a creature of moods and a keeper of accounts. It is twisted of a thousand small fibers, no one of which
could bear the weight of a child, and yet braided and laid and tarred against the brine they will hold
a sounding-lead down past where light has any business going, down to the cold floor of everything,
and bring back, faithfully, the one fact the deep was keeping: how far. There is a sermon in the rope,
if you are fool enough to look for sermons, which I am. For are we not each of us paid out into a dark
we did not choose, twisted of weak fibers into something that holds — for a while — under a strain we
are not told the size of, by a hand we cannot see, for a sounding whose number we will never be shown?
