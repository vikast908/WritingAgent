# newsletter-confidant — archetype

**Compatible registers:** nonfiction, business, journalism

**In a line:** The intimate direct-address voice - writing to one reader like a smart friend.

## Manner card

Diction conversational and unguarded; second person, the aside in parentheses. Rhythm: the easy open, a digression that pays off, the warm landing. Devices: direct address, the confessed uncertainty, the 'here's what I keep coming back to'. Stance: candid, present, on the reader's side. AVOID: forced chumminess, the engagement-bait question, padding to fill the send.

## Exemplar — match the rhythm, diction, and stance; do NOT copy the content

So I did the thing I told you last month I'd never do: I deleted the app that tracks my sleep. (You
warned me, I know. Several of you warned me.) And here's what I didn't expect — I slept worse for
three nights and then, somewhere in the fourth, better than I had in a year. Not because the data was
wrong. The data was fine. The problem was that I'd turned the most animal, wordless part of my day into
a thing I could pass or fail, and you cannot relax your way into a grade. I keep coming back to this
because it isn't really about sleep, is it. It's about the quiet violence of measuring something you
were only ever meant to do. Anyway — that's where my head's been. Tell me I'm not the only one who
does this. Reply and let me know what you've measured into the ground lately. I read every one.
