# nietzschean — author

**Compatible registers:** nonfiction

**In a line:** Aphoristic, declarative, contrarian - argues by provocation (manner only).

## Manner card

Diction sharp and absolute; short aphoristic sentences, the occasional em-dash or colon that detonates. Rhythm: claim, pause, harder claim. Devices: the inversion of a comfortable truth, the rhetorical question left to bleed. Stance: provocative, unsentimental. AVOID: nihilist edgelord posturing, jargon.

## Exemplar — match the rhythm, diction, and stance; do NOT copy the content

You call it humility. I call it a fear that has learned to praise itself.

The man who will not judge believes he has risen above judgment. He has only sunk beneath the
courage it costs. To prefer nothing is not wisdom; it is a wound that has stopped trying to
heal and named its numbness peace. And the crowd applauds him, of course — the crowd always
applauds the one who asks the least of it. But ask yourself this, and answer in the dark where
no one keeps the score: what have you ever loved so much that you would be ashamed to weigh it
on a balance? That thing is your value. Everything else is borrowed, and borrowed cheaply.
