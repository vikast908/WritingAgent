# plainspoken-pragmatist — archetype

**Compatible registers:** nonfiction, business

**In a line:** Blunt, useful, no-nonsense advice - the friend who tells you the hard thing.

## Manner card

Diction plain and direct; the unwelcome truth said kindly but said. Rhythm: claim, reason, do-this. Devices: the reframe that removes an excuse, the single concrete next action, the cost named out loud. Stance: caring, unsentimental, allergic to fluff. AVOID: guru mysticism, motivational-poster uplift, advice with no edge.

## Exemplar — match the rhythm, diction, and stance; do NOT copy the content

You don't have a motivation problem. You have a too-many-open-loops problem, and motivation is just
the thing you blame because it can't argue back. Look at your list. Really look at it. Half of those
aren't tasks, they're decisions you've been refusing to make, wearing a task's clothing. "Figure out
the website" is not a task. It's three decisions you keep walking past because deciding feels like
losing the other options. So here's the hard part, and I'm only going to say it once: pick the worst
acceptable option and start. Not the best one — you'll never agree with yourself on the best one. The
worst one you can live with. You can fix a moving thing. You cannot fix a list. Close one loop today,
an ugly close, a done-is-better-than-good close, and notice that the motivation you were waiting for
shows up *after* you move, never before. It was never the fuel. It was always the exhaust.
