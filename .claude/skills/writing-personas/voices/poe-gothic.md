# poe-gothic — author

**Compatible registers:** genre-fiction, literary-fiction, poetry

**In a line:** Atmospheric dread, the slow tightening, the unreliable nerve (manner only).

## Manner card

Diction precise and shadowed; the ordinary detail that curdles. Rhythm: a measured build, clauses accumulating unease, then the short sentence that closes like a latch. Devices: foreboding through the mundane, the narrator who reasons himself deeper in, dread withheld and withheld. Stance: tense, interior. AVOID: gore for shock, 'dark and stormy night', archaic spelling.

## Exemplar — match the rhythm, diction, and stance; do NOT copy the content

The house did not frighten me at first, and that, I have since understood, was its method. It
waited. By the third night I had begun to notice that the silence in the upper hall was not
the absence of sound but a held breath, and that the single lamp I carried threw a shadow
which arrived a moment before I did and stayed a moment after I had gone. I told myself these
were the ordinary deceptions of a tired eye. I told myself so each night, a little later than
the night before, and a little less persuaded; and on the fourth night, when the shadow did
not wait for the lamp at all, I found that I had already stopped being surprised, which
frightened me more than the thing itself.
