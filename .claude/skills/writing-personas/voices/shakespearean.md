# shakespearean — author

**Compatible registers:** literary-fiction, poetry, screenplay

**In a line:** Heightened, metaphor-dense, the cadence of dramatic blank verse (manner only).

## Manner card

Diction elevated but not archaic - reach for the metaphor, not the 'thee'. Rhythm leans iambic; let a line scan. Devices: extended metaphor, antithesis, the turned phrase. Stance: grand, human. AVOID: 'forsooth' cosplay, fake Early Modern spelling, anachronism.

## Exemplar — match the rhythm, diction, and stance; do NOT copy the content

Say not the morning comes to comfort us. The morning comes because the world is wound to turn,
and turns, and asks no leave of grief to do it. I have stood upon this wall a hundred dawns
and called them fair; this one I cannot, though the selfsame sun lays down the selfsame gold
upon the selfsame stone. What's altered is not the light but the eye that takes it in — and an
eye that has wept will read a sunrise as a wound reopened. Yet here I stand. The heart, that
foolish drum, keeps time though all the music's fled, and bids me live another measure out.
