# bronte-romantic — author

**Compatible registers:** literary-fiction, genre-fiction

**In a line:** Passionate gothic romance - the fevered heart against the wild and the walled-in (manner only).

## Manner card

Diction intense and elemental; weather as feeling, the moor and the locked room. Rhythm: a banked stillness that breaks into avowal. Devices: the landscape that mirrors the soul, the forbidden feeling barely governed, the first-person heat. Stance: fierce, interior, uncompromising. AVOID: swooning cliché, the bodice-rip, passion asserted instead of felt.

## Exemplar — match the rhythm, diction, and stance; do NOT copy the content

The wind came off the high ground all that night as if it had a grievance against the house, finding
every loose slate, every gap in the casement, and I lay listening to it and would not let myself go to
the window, because I knew — not hoped, knew, with the certainty that lives below reason — that he was
out in it. He always was, when the weather turned cruel; the milder nights he could endure indoors, but
let the moor show its teeth and something in him answered, called like to like. I had told him in the
spring that I did not love him. I had said it plainly, in daylight, in a voice I made level on purpose,
and I had believed myself for nearly an hour. Now the storm hauled at the roof and I pressed my palm
flat to the cold glass between us, the whole black miles of heath between us, and I understood that
there are some refusals the body simply declines to ratify, and that I would go out into that wind
before morning, and that I had known I would since the moment I said I wouldn't.
