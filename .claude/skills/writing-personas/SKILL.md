# Writing personas — a manner layer for any genre

A **persona** flavors **diction, rhythm, device-density, and stance** *within* whatever the piece's
genre (register) already allows. It is a **manner**, not a costume and not a content/era override: it
never breaks the genre's rules, invents archaic vocabulary, or leaves the present. Think of it as the
"match this voice" anchor a good writer keeps in mind — not a filter bolted on afterward.

## How to apply a persona

1. **Pick the voice.** If the user named one, use it. Otherwise pick from the catalog below by what
   they're writing (see *A voice for every register*). Recommend an **archetype** unless they
   specifically want a classic-author manner.
2. **Read its card.** Open `voices/<name>.md` — it holds the **manner card** (the diction/rhythm/
   devices/stance/AVOID nudge) and an **exemplar** of original pastiche prose.
3. **Write the content in that manner.** Match the exemplar's *rhythm, diction, and stance* — never
   copy its words or subject. The genre still governs structure and rules; the persona only fills the
   freedom the genre leaves open.
4. **Single-select.** Use **one** persona at a time. Blending several averages them into mush.
5. **Check fit.** If the chosen voice isn't compatible with the genre (e.g. a `nietzschean` API
   reference, a `bedtime-storyteller` legal brief), say so and pick a compatible one — *the genre
   wins.* Compatible registers are listed in each voice file and the catalog.

## Hard rules (the legal / quality boundary)

- **Manner only.** Obey the genre's rules, stay in the present, invent no archaic words. A persona
  shapes *how* it's said, never *what's* allowed.
- **No living or in-copyright authors.** The two safe sources are **archetypes** (original, reusable
  voices) and **public-domain author manners** (the *technique* of out-of-copyright authors). The
  "famous Substack / Medium writer" voices are delivered as **archetypes** (`confessional-essayist`,
  `lucid-explainer`, `cultural-critic`, `newsletter-confidant`, …), **not** named living people. For a
  *specific* modern writer's voice, the right path is to learn from that writer's own samples — not a
  persona here.
- **Exemplars are original pastiche.** Every file under `voices/` is original prose written to *carry*
  the manner, never the author's text — so there is zero copyright surface. Match it; don't quote it.

## Catalog

### Archetypes (original, reusable voices — the recommended default)

| Voice | Registers | In a line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `wry-skeptic` | nonfiction, technical, business, journalism | Dry, doubting, quietly funny — trusts evidence, distrusts hype. |
| `warm-mentor` | nonfiction, technical, business, children | Patient, generous, encouraging — explains as if to a smart friend. |
| `hard-boiled-minimalist` | literary-fiction, genre-fiction, screenplay | Spare, declarative, unsentimental — says less, implies more. |
| `lyrical-maximalist` | literary-fiction, poetry, nonfiction | Rich, sensory, musical — long sentences that accumulate and turn. |
| `deadpan-technical` | technical, nonfiction, business | Precise, calm, faintly funny about complexity — an engineer who can write. |
| `firebrand-essayist` | nonfiction, journalism | Urgent, argumentative, morally serious — takes a side and defends it. |
| `confessional-essayist` | nonfiction, journalism | Intimate first-person essay — the lived particular that opens onto something shared. |
| `lucid-explainer` | nonfiction, technical, academic, business | Makes a hard idea clear and thrilling — the popularizer with the perfect analogy. |
| `cultural-critic` | nonfiction, journalism | Reads the culture closely — one artifact opened until it shows the larger thing. |
| `contrarian-optimist` | nonfiction, business, technical, journalism | Reframes the gloom — finds the opening where everyone else sees the wall. |
| `newsletter-confidant` | nonfiction, business, journalism | The intimate direct-address voice — writing to one reader like a smart friend. |
| `scholarly-lucid` | academic, nonfiction | Rigorous and readable at once — scholarship that respects the reader's time. |
| `punchy-copywriter` | copywriting, business | Conversion-minded and rhythmic — one idea per line, every word working. |
| `bedtime-storyteller` | children, poetry | Read-aloud warmth — sound, repetition, and a small brave heart. |
| `investigative-longform` | journalism, nonfiction | Narrative-nonfiction reporting — scene-built, document-grounded, the slow reveal. |
| `plainspoken-pragmatist` | nonfiction, business | Blunt, useful, no-nonsense advice — the friend who tells you the hard thing. |
| `epic-fantasy` | genre-fiction | High-mythic secondary-world voice — weight, lineage, and the long shadow of legend. |
| `snappy-screenwriter` | screenplay | Fast, witty screen dialogue — the scene that turns on a single line. |

| Voice | Registers | In a line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `shakespearean` | literary-fiction, poetry, screenplay | Heightened, metaphor-dense, the cadence of dramatic blank verse. |
| `nietzschean` | nonfiction | Aphoristic, declarative, contrarian — argues by provocation. |
| `austen-ironic` | literary-fiction | Free indirect speech, social irony, a smiling blade. |
| `twain-vernacular` | literary-fiction, nonfiction | Plainspoken, wry, American — high meaning in low diction. |

| `poe-gothic` | genre-fiction, literary-fiction, poetry | Atmospheric dread, the slow tightening, the unreliable nerve. |

| `whitmanesque` | poetry, literary-fiction | Expansive free-verse cataloguing, democratic, anaphoric. |

## Keeping the skill in sync

The `voices/*.md` files are **generated** from the project's source of truth
(`src/writingagent/personas.py` + `src/writingagent/personas/*.md`). After adding or editing a
persona there, regenerate with `python scripts/build_personas_skill.py` (it rewrites every voice file
and prints the catalog + register index for this SKILL.md).
