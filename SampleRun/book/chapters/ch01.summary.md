## Chapter 1 Summary — "The Blank Envelope"

### Key Events
- Lena Voss, a rookie carrier of 11 weeks, delivers death-date envelopes on Hamlin Terrace in the Waterstone district, including to a new father holding an infant
- She delivers 9 envelopes, collects 19 thumbprints
- After her shift, senior carrier Olds mentions a new regulation requiring re-verification of death-date registrations due to a "discrepancy in the sorting algorithm"
- Lena voluntarily visits Records to retrieve her own birth envelope for proactive verification
- She opens her birth envelope in the reading room; the slip inside is completely blank — no date, no ink, no numbers
- A Misprint Alert sounds throughout the building
- Internal Affairs officers seize the slip and escort Lena to the Misprint Division Director's Office
- Director Wen informs Lena she is a "misprint" with no assigned death-date, making her functionally immortal and outside the system
- Wen offers Lena two choices: (1) join the Misprint Division as a "hunter" to retrieve other "broken daters" (defective individuals with corrupted predictions), or (2) submit to a "reprint" — being assigned a new death-date within the year
- Lena accepts the hunter position
- Auditor Ambrose Marchetti escorts her home to secure her quarters

### Character State Changes
- **Lena Voss**: Transitions from compliant, passive carrier/citizen to designated property of the Misprint Division; learns she is a "blank" — never assigned a death-date; accepts role as a hunter; status shifts from mortal to functionally immortal (provisionally)
- **Director Wen**: Revealed as head of Misprint Division; knows more than she discloses about Lena's situation
- **Auditor Marchetti**: Introduced as a looming, bureaucratic enforcer; now responsible for Lena's supervision

### New Facts Established
- Death-date envelopes are delivered at birth; recipients can request to open them at age 16
- Look-ahead petitions exist but are granted for <2% of cases
- Early death-date (<55) = "short-term resource" assigned to high-risk labor; late date (>80) = "long-term investment" with privileges
- The Algorithm is believed infallible; misprints are officially nonexistent
- Misprints (blank dates) are functionally immortal — cannot die of natural causes or conventional means
- The Bureau can "reprint" a blank, assigning an artificial death-date
- Misprint Division operates separately from standard Bureau; its symbol is a broken envelope line
- "Broken daters" are individuals with corrupted predictions who defect from the system and form underground networks
- The Alarm (Misprint Alert) signals detection of Algorithm errors

### Unresolved Threads
- Why was Lena's date skipped by the Algorithm? (Possibility of deliberate omission vs. genuine error?)
- What caused the broader "discrepancy in the sorting algorithm" mentioned by Olds?
- What is the full extent of the Bureau's misprint-related operations?
- Who are the broken daters, and what do they want?
- What does Auditor Marchetti know that he is not saying?
- Will Lena survive her first assignment? Is the Bureau's offer genuine or a trap?
- Does Lena have any family or connections who will notice her disappearance?
- What happens to the new father and his infant (foreshadowed subplot)?
